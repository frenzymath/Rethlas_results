# lemma lem:special-fiber-local-structure

## statement
Let
\[
  J=\bigcup_{n\ge 1}J_n,\qquad J_n=k[X,\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_n],
\]
with recurrence
\[
  X\zeta_n=\zeta_{n-1}^{s(n)}+\zeta_{n-2}\qquad(n\ge2),
\]
over the characteristic-zero field used in David's construction. Let \(A=J/XJ\), and write \(b_n\) for the image of \(\zeta_n\) in \(A\). Then
\[
  A=\bigcup_n k[b_n],\qquad b_{n-1}=-b_n^{s(n+1)}.
\]
If \(q\) is a nonzero prime of \(A\) which contains no \(b_n\), then \(q\) is maximal and \(A_q\) is a DVR. Moreover, if \(P\) is a height-two prime of \(J\) containing \(X\), then \(q=P/XJ\) is a nonzero maximal ideal of \(A\) containing no \(b_n\), and \((J_P)/(XJ_P)\cong A_q\) is a DVR.

## proof
Modulo \(X\), the recurrence gives \(b_{n-2}=-b_{n-1}^{s(n)}\). Hence the image of \(J_n=k[X,\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_n]\) in \(A\) is \(k[b_n]\), and the transition \(k[b_n]\hookrightarrow k[b_{n+1}]\) sends \(b_n\) to \(-b_{n+1}^{s(n+2)}\).

Let \(q\subset A\) be nonzero and contain no \(b_n\). For every \(n\), the contraction \(q_n=q\cap k[b_n]\) is a nonzero prime of \(k[b_n]\). Indeed, after choosing a nonzero element of \(q\) in some finite stage, integrality of all transition maps makes the later contractions nonzero; the earlier contractions cannot be zero because then a field \(k[b_m]/q_m\) would be integral over the nonfield \(k[b_n]\). Thus \(q_n\) is maximal in \(k[b_n]\); write \(q_n=(f_n(b_n))\), with \(f_n(0)\ne0\). Since \(A/q=\bigcup_n k[b_n]/q_n\) is a directed union of fields along field embeddings, \(A/q\) is a field, so \(q\) is maximal.

Set \(R_n=k[b_n]_{q_n}\). The local map \(R_n\to R_{n+1}\) is finite and, at \(q_{n+1}\), is unramified: the defining equation is \(b_n+b_{n+1}^e=0\), where \(e=s(n+2)\), and its derivative \(e b_{n+1}^{e-1}\) is a unit because the characteristic is zero and \(b_{n+1}\notin q_{n+1}\). Therefore the ramification index is one, and a uniformizer of one \(R_n\) remains a uniformizer in every later \(R_m\). It follows that \(A_q=\bigcup_n R_n\) is a DVR: every nonzero element lies in some \(R_n\), hence has a well-defined integral order with respect to this common uniformizer, and every nonzero ideal is generated by an element of minimal order.

Now let \(P\supset XJ\) be height two and put \(q=P/XJ\). If \(q=0\), then \(P=XJ\), since \(XJ\) is prime, contrary to \({\rm ht}(P)=2\). If \(q\) contains some \(b_n\), then the relations force it to contain every \(b_m\), so \(q=(b_0,b_1,\ldots)\). Its inverse image is David's origin maximal ideal \((X,\zeta_0,\zeta_1,\ldots)J\), which David proves has height three. Hence a height-two \(P\) cannot occur in this way. Thus \(q\) is nonzero and contains no \(b_n\), and the preceding paragraph gives \(J_P/XJ_P\cong A_q\) a DVR.

The David facts just used are part of the following external result. **External result (David, paper_id David1973, theorem_id Corollary 1.1, Lemma 2.3, Theorem 3, and Corollary 4.1, arXiv id not applicable).** For the characteristic-zero ring \(J\) constructed above, \(X\) is a prime element, \(J\) is a three-dimensional factorial domain, \(J_{(X)}\) is a rank-one DVR, and the origin maximal ideal \((X,\zeta_0,\zeta_1,\ldots)J\) has height three.

# lemma lem:height-two-localizations-are-noetherian

## statement
If \(P\) is a height-two prime of \(J\) containing \(X\), then \(J_P\) is a two-dimensional Noetherian local domain. In particular \(J_P\) is Jaffard.

## proof
Set \(S=J_P\), \(M=PS\), and \(q=P/XJ\). By Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:special\text{-}fiber\text{-}local\text{-}structure}\), \(q\) is a nonzero maximal ideal of \(A=J/XJ\) containing no \(b_n\), and \(A_q\cong S/XS\) is a DVR.

For each \(n\), write \(q_n=(f_n(b_n))\). The contraction of \(P\) to \(J_n=k[X,\zeta_{n-1},\zeta_n]\) is
\[
  P_n=(X,\,\zeta_{n-1}+\zeta_n^{s(n+1)},\,f_n(\zeta_n))J_n.
\]
This is the inverse image of \(q_n\) under \(J_n\to A\). Choose one index \(N\) and put \(t=f_N(\zeta_N)\in S\). We claim that
\[
  M=(X,t)S.
\]
Indeed, every element of \(P\) lies in some \(P_n\). The generator \(\zeta_{n-1}+\zeta_n^{s(n+1)}\) is equal in \(S\) to \(X\zeta_{n+1}\), hence belongs to \(XS\). The image of \(f_n(\zeta_n)\) in \(S/XS\cong A_q\) is a uniformizer of the DVR \(A_q\), as is the image of \(t\). Therefore these two images differ by a unit of \(A_q\). Lifting that unit to a unit of the local ring \(S\), we get \(f_n(\zeta_n)\in (X,t)S\). Hence each \(P_nS\subseteq (X,t)S\), and the reverse inclusion is clear.

We now use Cohen's theorem: a commutative ring is Noetherian if and only if all of its prime ideals are finitely generated. Every prime of \(S\) is finitely generated. The zero ideal is finitely generated. The maximal ideal is \(M=(X,t)S\). If \(H\) is a nonzero nonmaximal prime of \(S\), then \({\rm ht}(H)=1\) because \(S\) is a two-dimensional local domain. Let \(H_0=H\cap J\). Then \(H_0\subsetneq P\), \(H=H_0S\), and \(H_0\ne0\). Since \({\rm ht}_J(P)=2\), the strict inclusion \(H_0\subsetneq P\) forces \({\rm ht}_J(H_0)=1\); otherwise a length-two chain below \(H_0\) followed by \(H_0\subsetneq P\) would give \({\rm ht}_J(P)\ge3\). Thus \(H_0\) is a height-one prime of the factorial domain \(J\), hence is generated by a prime element; localizing at \(P\) shows that \(H\) is principal in \(S\). Thus all primes of \(S\) are finitely generated, so \(S\) is Noetherian by Cohen's theorem. Since \(S\) is a finite-dimensional Noetherian domain, it is Jaffard.

# theorem thm:target

## statement
A finite-dimensional integral domain $D$ is said to be a \emph{Jaffard domain} if $\dim(D[X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n]) = n + \dim(D)$ for all $n \geq 1$; equivalently, if $\dim(D) = \dim_v(D)$, where $\dim(D)$ denotes the (Krull) dimension of $D$ and $\dim_v(D)$ its valuative dimension. $D$ is said to be a \emph{locally Jaffard domain} if $D_P$ is Jaffard for each prime ideal $P$ of $D$.

In [16], Bouchiba and Kabbaj examined David's second construction described in [29] of a $3$-dimensional factorial domain which arises as an ascending union of $3$-dimensional polynomial rings $J_n$ in three variables over a field $k$; namely, $J = \bigcup J_n$ with $J_n = k[X, \zeta_{n-1}, \zeta_n]$ for each positive integer $n$, where the variables $\zeta_n$ satisfy the following condition: For $n \geq 2$, $\zeta_n = \frac{\zeta_{n-1}^{s(n)} + \zeta_{n-2}}{X}$, where the $s(n)$ are positive integers. We have $J_n \subseteq J \subseteq J_n[X^{-1}]$ for each positive integer $n$. Therefore, by [30, Theorem 2.3], $J$ is a Jaffard domain (since the $J_n$'s are affine domains, i.e., finitely generated $k$-algebras), but it is not known if $J$ is locally Jaffard. A negative answer will solve (affirmatively) Bouvier's conjecture, which asserts that finite-dimensional Krull (or, more particularly, factorial) domains need not be Jaffard.

[16] S. Bouchiba and S. Kabbaj, Bouvier's conjecture, Commutative Algebra and its Applications, de Gruyter, Berlin, 2009, 79-88.

[29] J. David, A characteristic zero non-Noetherian factorial ring of dimension three, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 180 (1973), 315-325.

[30] D. E. Dobbs, M. Fontana and S. Kabbaj, Direct limits of Jaffard domains and S-domains, Comment. Math. Univ. St. Pauli 39 (2) (1990), 143-155.

Is $J$ (David's 3-dimensional factorial domain) a locally Jaffard domain?

## proof
Yes. We prove that every localization of \(J\) at a prime ideal is Jaffard.

We use the following external result already quoted in the problem statement. **External result (Dobbs--Fontana--Kabbaj, paper_id DobbsFontanaKabbaj1990, theorem_id Theorem 2.3, arXiv id not applicable).** If a directed union \(D=\bigcup_n D_n\) of affine Jaffard domains satisfies \(D_n\subseteq D\subseteq D_n[u^{-1}]\) for a fixed nonzero element \(u\) and all \(n\), then \(D\) is Jaffard. Applied to David's sequence \(J_n\subseteq J\subseteq J_n[X^{-1}]\), this gives \(\dim_v(J)=\dim(J)=3\).

Let \(P\in\operatorname{Spec}(J)\). If \(X\notin P\), then
\[
  J_P=(J[X^{-1}])_{PJ[X^{-1}]}=(J_n[X^{-1}])_{PJ_n[X^{-1}]}
\]
for any \(n\). This is a localization of a three-variable polynomial ring over \(k\), hence is Noetherian and therefore Jaffard.

Assume now that \(X\in P\). Since \(XJ\) is prime, the height-one case is \(P=XJ\), and David's Lemma 2.3 says \(J_P=J_{(X)}\) is a rank-one DVR; hence it is Jaffard. If \({\rm ht}(P)=3\), then \(\dim(J_P)=3\), while localization cannot increase valuative dimension, so
\[
  \dim_v(J_P)\le \dim_v(J)=3.
\]
The opposite inequality \(\dim_v(J_P)\ge\dim(J_P)=3\) is general, hence \(J_P\) is Jaffard.

The only remaining case is \({\rm ht}(P)=2\) with \(X\in P\). This is exactly Lemma \(\mathrm{lem:height\text{-}two\text{-}localizations\text{-}are\text{-}noetherian}\). Therefore \(J_P\) is Jaffard for every prime \(P\), and \(J\) is locally Jaffard.
